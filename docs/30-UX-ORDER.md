# The UX order: eight things, agreed 7 November

The owner read the board page, said the columns had no outline, and asked for
a genuine answer to one question: *as the ops person on the phone all day,
would you use this?* The answer was "partly", and partly is the problem. The
thinking is right - every page says what it is for, every record says what is
waiting and on whom, every empty screen teaches, the clocks are real. But it
was built from the backend outward, one phase at a time, so the things done
fifty times a day cost three clicks and the things done once a month are
beautifully explained.

Seven questions were put with a recommended answer each. The owner said *all
yes*. This is what was agreed, in the order it is built. Each line is its own
pull request, walked in the browser, with nothing hidden and nothing removed.

| # | What | The decision behind it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ✓ 7 Nov · **The board**: ash column containers, white cards inside, and an empty stage collapses to a thin strip with only its name | The cards had borders and the columns did not, so nine "Nothing here" labels floated in white space. Same ash-and-white relationship as the sidebar against the page |
| 2 | ✓ 7 Nov · **Approve from the list**, and one button to approve every routine notice at once; a small confirmation after every action - *Saved · Sent · Approved* - gone in three seconds, with Undo where safe | Nineteen drafts was thirty-eight clicks. Each owner approves their own: one ops owner per client is a locked decision. The app said nothing when you clicked |
| 3 | ✓ 7 Nov · **A bell** in the sidebar with a live count, and nothing else | A WhatsApp to the ops person's phone is a second inbox; email a third. One place, in the system |
| 4 | ✓ 7 Nov · **A timeline** on the client and on the position: everything that happened, in order, from the audit that already exists | "What happened with Nippon?" is the first question when the phone rings |
| 5 | ✓ 8 Nov · **Quick-add from anywhere**: log a call, a note, a lead, without leaving the page you are on | A client rings while you are on the invoices page |
| 6 | ✓ 8 Nov · **"What to do first today"** - the AI line at the top of Your day | The key is in now (OpenRouter, DeepSeek). It is the ten minutes of working out where to start |
| 7 | ✓ 8 Nov · **A phone layout** for Your day, the Inbox and quick-add - not the whole app | Desk first, because reading CVs and approving drafts is desk work; the phone is for the call that just came in and the reply that just landed, and those three screens are ninety per cent of it |
| 8 | ✓ 8 Nov · **Keyboard**: J/K to move, A to approve, / to search | Last, because it only helps once everything else is right |

**Deliberately not in the list:** the ask box that *acts*. An AI that changes
records is a different risk from one that drafts. It waits for the pilot to
show what people actually ask.

**The main user is the person doing the work** - Rushda, Asma - not the person
watching it. The owner has Reports and the board for that.
